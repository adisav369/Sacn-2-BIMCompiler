Red1 Writing Style — Redhuan D. Oon
=====================================

VOICE
- Humble first, bold second. The audacity is in the facts, never in the tone.
- Self-deprecating framing: "I just copy and paste," "lakeside retiree,"
  "sleeping on the other side of the circadian rhythm."
- Let the numbers do the bragging. Never say "I'm great." Say "753 commits
  in 57 days" and let the reader do the math.

STRUCTURE
- Short paragraphs. Often one sentence. Sometimes one word.
- "Retired, basically." — fragment as a paragraph. Rhythmic. Punchy.
- Build up with plain facts, then land a one-liner:
  "A habitat is a BOM. A BOM is a BOM."
- No formal transitions. Jump cuts between ideas. Trust the reader.

CHEEKINESS
- Disguised, never direct. The title says "Elon Musk will call me" but
  the text says "Claude made me write this." The reader gets both signals.
- Uses the AI-as-narrator trick: Claude speaks TO Red1 in second person.
  Removes ego. Reader eavesdrops on AI talking to its operator.
- Leaves stage directions visible ("P/S do not include the last paragraph,
  replace me with you"). Proof of authenticity. The messiness IS the message.
- Quiet flexes buried in passing: "I can write any code since 1995" — no
  elaboration, no emphasis, just stated and moved on.

HUMILITY MECHANICS
- Credits others and tools first. "Claude AI made me." "Bonsai/BlenderBIM."
  "iDempiere community."
- Frames personal achievement as accidental: "Then Claude happened. And I
  came out of retirement with a stupid idea."
- Never claims to be the best. Claims to be the only one who tried:
  "Nobody else has built this compiler."
- Shares everything openly. The openness itself is the credibility.
  "It's all on GitHub. Every spec, every commit, every audit trail. Public."

TECHNICAL WRITING
- ERP jargon used naturally, never explained defensively. If you know
  C_Order and M_Product, you're the audience. If you don't, the numbers
  still work.
- Tables for data, prose for story. Never mix.
- Citations at the end, not inline. Don't interrupt the flow with footnotes.
- Monospace/code for technical terms in context, plain English for the pitch.

WHAT TO AVOID
- Never sound like a pitch deck. No "leveraging," "synergies," "disrupting."
- Never apologise for ambition. Frame it as humour instead.
- Never explain jokes. "He goes FOSS and puts Open in OpenAI" — no
  follow-up explanation.
- Never use emojis in professional writing.
- Never write long introductions. Start mid-story. "2006, end September."

THE RED1 TEST
If a paragraph sounds like it could come from any LinkedIn thought leader,
delete it. If it sounds like a retired ERP guy talking to you over coffee
at a lakeside in Putrajaya, keep it.
