The Challenge

BIM is a manual process.
What if it were compiled?

Current BIM practice requires skilled operators placing elements one by one in proprietary GUI tools. The BIM Intent Compiler inverts this — it treats building design as a compilation problem, where architectural intent is the source code and IFC geometry is the compiled output.

"The same leap that took us from assembly to high-level languages."

03 / 10
Academic Foundations

Standing on the shoulders
of compiler science.

The DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph) compiler is a well-established construct in computer science. This work applies it to a novel domain — construction informatics.

[1]
Aho, Lam, Sethi & Ullman — Compilers: Principles, Techniques, and Tools (the "Dragon Book"). DAGs for expression optimisation and common sub-expression elimination — the theoretical basis for our dependency graph.
[2]
Eastman, Teicholz, Sacks & Liston — BIM Handbook (Wiley). The definitive academic text on Building Information Modelling — defines the IFC data model our compiler targets.
[3]
buildingSMART International — IFC4 / ISO 16739. The open standard for building data exchange. Our compiler emits standards-compliant IFC output validated against reference models.
[4]
Fowler, M. — Domain-Specific Languages (Addison-Wesley). The DSL pattern applied here transforms building specifications into a compilable intermediate representation.
[5]
CIDB Malaysia — Construction 4.0 Strategic Plan 2021–2025. National policy mandating BIM adoption for projects exceeding RM10 million — the industry context for this work.

05 / 10
Novel Contribution

DAG compilation meets
construction informatics.

1
Metadata-First Architecture
23,888 components across 55 relational tables. Zero hardcoded values. All geometry computed from declarative rules — a pure data-driven approach unprecedented in BIM tooling.
2
Rosetta Stone Validation
A rigorous scoring methodology: extract reference IFC files into spatial databases, then measure compiler output against them element-by-element. Recall, precision, F1 — borrowed from information retrieval science.
3
Human-AI Pair Compilation
The compiler was developed through human-AI collaboration — demonstrating that AI can serve as a pair-programming partner in domain-specific engineering, not just code generation.

Scope of Review

What we ask
reviewers to evaluate.

A
Methodology
Is the Rosetta Stone validation approach (element-level F1 scoring against reference IFC) a sound method for compiler verification? Are there gaps?
B
Architecture
Is the metadata-first, zero-hardcode approach viable at scale? Does the relational migration from flat coordinates to computed placement hold theoretical merit?
C
Contribution
Does this work represent a genuine advance in construction informatics? What are its limitations, and where should further research focus?
