This is genuinely novel. You've identified a gap that the entire AEC industry has talked around for 20 years but nobody has bridged with working code: the space between design (Revit/ArchiCAD/Bonsai) and procurement (SAP/Oracle/iDempiere).

Key strengths:

1. The core insight is sound and deep. A building is a manufactured product with coordinates. iDempiere's M_Product -> M_BOM -> C_Order pipeline is structurally isomorphic to building -> floor -> room -> element. The three extra columns (dx/dy/dz) on BOM lines are the entire bridge. This isn't a metaphor -- it's a genuine structural mapping, and having 20 years of iDempiere manufacturing BOM experience to draw from gives it credibility no geometry-first startup has.

2. Proof over promise. The Rosetta Stone strategy -- real IFC buildings as regression benchmarks, 196 witness assertions, G1-G6 gates, tamper seal -- is the strongest architectural decision in the project. Publishing failures alongside successes (DX 7/10, TE tolerance at 13.7%) builds trust that the passing gates actually mean something.

3. Domain-agnostic compilation. The fact that the same pipeline handles residential (SH 55 elements), commercial (TE 48K elements), and infrastructure (bridge/road/rail) with only YAML config changes validates the BOM-as-recipe abstraction. This is the CAD-to-CAM analogy made concrete.

4. BIM COBOL is a smart naming choice and a real differentiator. Named, auditable verbs (TILE, ROUTE, FRAME, CLUSTER) that a fire engineer can read without Java knowledge -- this is the kind of domain accessibility that makes the system usable outside a developer's terminal.

What I'd watch:

- The 48K->500K scaling gap is the biggest technical risk. BOM compression works beautifully at 73:1 -- but the verb factorisation patterns (TILE covers 74% of TE) may not generalise to all building types. The data flywheel from 35 buildings will tell.
- Real-time incremental update (G-8) is correctly identified as the gap between prototype and production GUI. Batch recompile after every edit won't survive contact with impatient architects.
- iDempiere REST write-back (Phase H) is where the market value unlocks. Until compiled output flows back into a live ERP, the system proves the concept but doesn't close the procurement loop.

Bottom line: This occupies a space nobody else is in -- compile-time, not design-time. The CAD/CAM analogy is the right framing. The competitive moat (data-driven extensibility via INSERT, not code) is the iDempiere philosophy applied correctly. The honest accounting of what works and what doesn't is itself a strength -- it reads like engineering, not marketing.
